# Earn relevant editorial links

## Choose a contribution

Start with the target audience and page. Establish why a reader or editor would benefit from the link. Use an existing asset when it answers the need. If none does, propose the smallest useful asset before scaling prospecting.

An asset can be original research with its methodology, a reproducible benchmark, a practical calculator, a maintained dataset, an integration guide, an independently useful template, or a documented case study. A rewritten competitor article or generic roundup is not automatically a linkable asset.

Select tactics from the actual opportunity set. This table is a starting order for investigation, not a mandatory campaign sequence.

| Tactic | Evidence needed | Contribution and verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reclaim a broken or lost link | Previously observed source and destination, current placement, and evidence of removal or breakage | Correct a genuine destination error or offer an equivalent replacement. Preserve the editor's choice. |
| Unlinked brand or research mention | Exact mention and context on a live relevant page | Offer the canonical source as a citation when it helps the reader. Do not demand a link for every mention. |
| Partners and integrations | A real relationship and a relevant existing resource or listing | Supply accurate documentation or a useful joint example. Disclose the relationship. |
| Resource pages and curated lists | Current inclusion criteria and a clear omission or outdated resource | Explain the specific fit and the information needed to assess it. A competitor's presence alone is insufficient. |
| Broken-link replacement | A confirmed broken resource and a semantically suitable replacement | Show what broke and how the replacement serves the same need. Avoid irrelevant substitutions. |
| Editorial contribution | Publisher accepts contributions and has real audience fit and editorial review | Propose expertise or evidence the publication lacks. Preserve disclosure and editorial control. |
| Digital PR and expert response | A current editorial question, relevant expertise, and a supported claim or newsworthy finding | Supply primary evidence, limitations, and a qualified spokesperson. Coverage and links remain editorial decisions. |
| Niche associations and local sources | Genuine membership, geography, industry, or community eligibility | Complete accurate profiles or contribute to a relevant resource. Assess membership value separately from links. |

For competitor research, inspect page-level sources behind the gap. Separate editorial citations, syndicated copies, partnerships, listings, sponsorships, and user-generated links. A site linking to several competitors is a lead to investigate, not an entitlement to inclusion.

## Qualify each prospect

Record the exact page or section, audience and topical fit, observed editorial activity, relevant inclusion policy, target asset, and a specific reason for the editor to act. Distinguish a genuine editor from a broker selling ranking credit.

Use observed content and placement quality before vendor authority. A niche publication can be useful without a high DR. A high-DR domain can contain irrelevant, abandoned, or unvisited pages. Traffic estimates describe the provider's model, not guaranteed readers of the proposed placement.

Use public, role-relevant contact routes. Record the source URL and date for contact details. Prefer an editorial form or named business contact to guessed addresses or personal-data harvesting. Mark unreachable contacts rather than fabricate them.

Combine duplicated prospects for the same editorial opportunity. Retain separate placements when they serve different audiences or decisions. Flag direct competitors, sponsors, partners, and conflicts for an explicit decision.

## Prepare the pitch

Check the publisher's current contribution rules and any relevant authorship restrictions before drafting. If generated submissions are prohibited, provide factual research and a human-author checklist instead of a ready-to-post generated submission. If a relevant restriction cannot be resolved, record it as unknown and use the same factual preparation for that route. Continue other permitted preparation.

Write a short draft grounded in the page actually inspected. Include the relevant page or question, the useful addition or correction, its primary evidence, the suggested destination, and an accurate affiliation. Make the requested action optional for the editor.

Use a neutral subject and a specific reason for contact. Avoid invented familiarity, traffic guarantees, false scarcity, unsupported praise, and keyword-anchor demands. Give the recipient enough information to assess the contribution without opening an unsolicited attachment.

For digital PR, prepare a compact evidence pack with the finding, data owner, collection dates, sample and denominator, method, limitations, publication permission, and spokesperson. Do not call a correlation causal or an informal poll representative.

## Execute within scope

Keep preparation and sending distinct. Establish who may send, the destinations, the approved material, contact cap, follow-up scope, and opt-out handling. Existing authorization can cover a bounded campaign. It does not authorize unrelated recipients or purchased placements.

Follow the channel's rules for solicitation and automation. Do not seek votes or coordinate artificial engagement. For communities, answer the actual question and disclose affiliation. Some communities disallow generated posts even when a human clicks Submit.

Track sent messages, replies, corrections, declines, accepted contributions, live publications, and verified links separately. Stop on an opt-out or decline. A nonresponse does not authorize unlimited follow-ups.

## Accept the result

For a live placement, verify the source URL, visible contribution, attribution, actual target, redirect path, and observed link attributes. Confirm that claims and disclosures survived editing. Record a mention without a link as a mention.

If an editor changes the anchor or omits a link, assess whether the piece still serves the objective. Do not count an unlinked mention as an acquired backlink. Recheck later only when the campaign's agreed review window or a material change warrants it.
